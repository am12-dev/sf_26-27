1. The overlapping-cycle defect

Two of the six survey cycles are the same women

CYCLES contains both 2017_2018 (NHANES letter J) and 2017_2020 (prefix P_). The NHANES pre-pandemic release is 2017–2018 combined with 2019–March 2020 — field work stopped in March 2020, so NCHS pooled the partial 2019–2020 sample with the complete 2017–2018 cycle to restore national representativeness. The 2017–2018 respondents are physically inside the P_ files. CDC sanctions pooling the pre-pandemic files with 1999–2016, not with 2017–2018.

A sequence-number check does not catch it. DEMO_J runs SEQN 93703–102956 (n = 9,254); P_DEMO runs 109263–124822 (n = 15,560). The ranges are disjoint, so SEQN.duplicated() returns nothing. The people are nonetheless the same, which three independent checks establish:

  • All 176 women of the 1,484 cohort drawn from 2017_2018 match a woman in 2017_2020 exactly on femoral neck BMD, total hip BMD, age, weight and height. The same test between 2009–2010 and either cycle returns 0.
  • At whole-file scale, 7,585 of 7,585 distinct BMXWT+BMXHT+BMXBMI+BMXWAIST fingerprints in BMX_J also occur in P_BMX — every one.
  • The matched pairs agree on 176 of 176 for bmi, diabetes, any_prior_fracture, steroid_use, postmenopausal, years_since_menopause and ever_used_estrogen.

Two consequences go beyond the counts:

  1. The cross-validation leaks. StratifiedKFold splits rows, not people. For 161 postmenopausal women an identical row can sit in a training fold while its twin is scored in the test fold — exact-duplicate leakage across 12.7% of the cohort. That inflates every cross-validated AUC, including the nested-CV figure we describe as the defensible one.
  2. The published table is superseded. 0.810 and 0.806 were computed on contaminated folds and on 1,272 / 91. The apply_filters assert at 1,484 will trip — which is the assert performing its stated function, not a bug.

It also widens the Q8 margin retroactively: the osteoporosis target carries 76 events, not 91, so carrying T ≤ −2.5 by thresholding the T-score regression was right by more than was shown at the time.

Why both cycles were in the list

Serum vitamin D. Among the 176 matched pairs the J copy carries 173 serum values and the P_ copy carries 0 — VID was never released for the pre-pandemic file. The 973-woman serum arm depends on those rows.

Ruling — Option 1 plus the crosswalk

  • Drop 2017_2018 from CYCLES. 1,484 → 1,308 records, all distinct; the leakage goes; P_ retains the additional 2019–March 2020 women.
  • Recover serum vitamin D by crosswalk, for the vitamin D arm only. Graft the VID_J values onto their matched P_ records using the BMD + age + weight + height fingerprint. This is a non-standard step and must be named plainly in methods, with the match rule and the count recovered stated, so a reviewer can see exactly what was joined and on what.
  • Re-run everything. Every n and every AUC in this document and in the published table predates the deduplication and must be recomputed before use.

The weighting question cannot be answered as asked. The two-year cycles carry WTMEC2YR; P_ carries WTMECPRP on a 3.2-year basis. The cycle set is non-contiguous (2011–2012 absent, 2015–2016 deliberately excluded) and, until the fix above, overlapping. NHANES pooled-weight guidance assumes contiguous, non-overlapping cycles sharing a design stratification, so no valid pooled weight exists for this set. See Q11.

2. The twelve decisions

Each block gives the question as put, the specialist's answer verbatim, and the ruling being built. Cohort sizes quoted are pre-deduplication and are subject to §1.

Q1 — What is the outcome we are predicting? overrides specialist

Asked

Three candidates: low bone mass or worse (T ≤ −1.0, 751 of 1,272), osteoporosis (T ≤ −2.5, 91), or the T-score as a number. Which is primary?

Specialist answered

T-score

Ruling

The binary is primary, with T ≤ −1.0 as the headline. Classifiers lead; the T-score is reported alongside. This departs from the written answer and the document must record that it was overridden deliberately, with the reason: the clinical question is a referral decision, and a binary primary with a continuous secondary is what the reporting conventions in this space expect.

Q2 — Which bone site defines the T-score? locked

Asked

Femoral neck only (722 / 81), total hip only (495 / 43), or the lower of the two (751 / 91)? Both sites exist for all 1,272, so the choice is definitional.

Specialist answered

Use the lowest of the 2 sites, but run prediction model for both sites

Ruling

  • Lowest-of-two is primary.
  • Femoral-neck-only and total-hip-only run as pre-specified sensitivity analyses, headline models only — not the full leaderboard.
  • The ablation grid runs once, on lowest-of-two.
  • A Riley minimum computed per target gates whether an arm is modelled or reported descriptively.
Q3 — Is 1,272 the group we report? locked

Asked

Removing anyone with a cycle in the last twelve months drops 170 women who reported a period and a further 42 whose status cannot be determined at all. Confirm 1,272?

Specialist answered

Yes — we report on 1272.

Ruling

Confirmed. The 42 indeterminate women stay excluded, but methods must state they were dropped because their status could not be determined — not because they were premenopausal — and must show their characteristics. They are statistically indistinguishable from the retained cohort (mean T −1.167 against −1.162; age 58 against 59), so this is a missing-data rule bundled inside a cohort definition and should be visible as such.

Q4 — Repeat on all 1,484? And how is years-since-menopause defined after hysterectomy? locked

Asked

Two parts. Adding the 212 excluded women makes the mix easier independently of the model (age alone rises 0.555 → 0.588; OST 0.733 → 0.747). And of the 1,272, 460 report a hysterectomy and 206 removal of the ovaries, for whom the menopause date is the operation.

Specialist answered

(left blank — settled verbally)

Ruling — part A

No repeat run on 1,484. A limitations line records that the 212 were never used.

Ruling — part B

  • No menopause-type variable. The specialist wants no natural / surgical / hysterectomy distinction in the model. Postmenopausal status is defined once, by the gate: no period in the past 12 months (RHQ031), which gives the analysis cohort of 1,111.
  • Years since menopause (age − RHQ060) is kept. It is observed for 1,089 of the 1,111; the other 22 receive the fold-internal median.

Revised 2026-10-02. An earlier version of this ruling added a four-level menopause_type (3 indicator columns) and a rebuilt menopause_natural; both were removed from the pipeline. In the ablation they contributed nothing (ΔAUC −0.000 [−0.003, +0.003]). The residual caveat stays as a stated limitation: after a hysterectomy, “years since last period” can mean years since surgery.

Q5 — How much detail on estrogen use? locked

Asked

Yes/no for 1,270; usable duration for 272 of 532 users. Yes/no only, duration added, or both?

Specialist answered

Primary – Y/N, secondary less than 2 years vs > 2 years

Ruling

  • Primary: ever-use Y/N.
  • Secondary: four levels — never / <2 y / ≥2 y / duration unknown — on the full 1,272, so no one is silently dropped.
  • Derivation coalesces all three routes: RHQ560Q/U (pills), RHQ576Q/U (patch), RHQ586Q/U (combined), covering 376 users.

The 272 → 376 correction goes back to the specialist (errata 2).

Q6 — Depression score: subset analysis only? locked

Asked

The earlier “20% available” was a coding error — the nine PHQ items are stored as words in five of six cycles. Corrected, the score exists for 1,271 of 1,272, and 127 (10.0%) score 10 or above. Main analysis? And the threshold as yes/no alongside?

Specialist answered

Yes and Yes

Ruling

  • Continuous phq9_score in the main feature set.
  • phq9_ge10 as a substitution arm — never both in one model. A score and its own dichotomisation in the same design is collinear by construction and makes both coefficients uninterpretable.
  • The 10.0% positive rate appears in the demographic table.
Q7 — Rheumatoid arthritis: predictor or description? locked

Asked

Only 49 women have RA, and 2009–10 was thought not to type arthritis, leaving 98 unclassifiable. Predictor with an explicit unknown category, or demographic table only?

Specialist answered

Subset analysis for the group who have answered that they have RA or no RA (we just need to trust their answer)

Ruling

  • Keep the subset ruling: the RA arm runs on 1,142 women with 84 cases — not 49 (errata 3 and 4).
  • Descriptive only at T ≤ −2.5.
  • The non-random exclusion of the 130 must be stated wherever the arm appears. Those women are not missing at random — mean T −0.934, better than every other group — so the 1,142 arm carries a harder case mix than the main cohort and its AUC is not comparable to the headline.
Q8 — Diabetes runs opposite to the prediction locked

Asked

Mean T −0.807 in diabetics against −1.211 in non-diabetics; the relationship disappears once age and BMI are accounted for (p = 0.79). Only 7 osteoporosis cases among diabetics. Does describing side-by-side, testing the BMI interaction, and building in non-diabetics match what you want?

Specialist answered

check if sample size of DM too less to be of significance

…and, separately, advised that diabetes is a predictor of low bone mass.

Ruling

  • Diabetes stays a predictor, judged by the with/without ablation rather than by the direction of its coefficient. Dropping a variable because it went the wrong way is the sign-fishing the diagnostics exist to catch.
  • No diabetes entry in EXPECTED_SIGNS. Adding +1 on clinical intuition would fire the leak alarm on a correct finding.
  • The diabetic bone paradox — higher BMD alongside higher fracture risk, which a T-score cannot show — goes near the front of limitations, not buried in it.
  • A pre-specified −0.5 SD T-score offset sensitivity arm for diabetic women.
  • State that type 1 and type 2 cannot be separated in this survey.
  • The 33 borderline cases fold into non-diabetic (1,142), with a reclassification sensitivity run.
  • Descriptive side-by-side plus an interaction test on 1,272; modelling in non-diabetics only.
  • T ≤ −2.5 is carried by thresholding the T-score regression, so that all 1,272 women contribute information rather than only the 91 positives. The 91-case classifier is retained in METHODS as a labelled comparison.

Ruling

  • Collapse the ordinal to binary — parental hip fracture Y/N is the modelled feature. The 0 / 1 / 2 table is shown descriptively with the n = 6 stated. This departs from the written answer.
  • Keep both features — parental hip fracture and first-degree family history of osteoporosis — and let the ablation judge them.
  • Drop parental fracture before 50 — 16 women (7 mothers, 9 fathers). Descriptive footnote at most.
  • State in advance, as with Q9, that family history is expected to add little.

Two implementation notes

  • The coding defect appears a fifth time. P_OSQ is numeric (1/2/7/9) while the other five cycles are label-decoded ("Yes"/"No"/"Don't know"). Any string-only match silently drops all 264 women in 2017–2020. After MCQ195 and MCQ191 this is a pattern and wants one shared yes/no helper, not a fifth bespoke fix.
  • REFUSED is unsafe on the age-at-parental-fracture fields. OSQ180 has 149 non-null cohort values, of which 2 are the 999 sentinel — giving 147, exactly the specialist's figure. But the global REFUSED = [7, 9, 77, 99, 777, 999, 7777, 9999] at OsteoporosisML.py:90 strips it to 144, because 77 and 99 are plausible ages for a mother's hip fracture. These columns need a column-specific sentinel rule.
Q11 — Should we use the survey's population weights? needs restating

Asked

Weights make results represent the US population; omitting them describes only the women sampled. Our suggestion was unweighted for prediction work, since the goal is ranking individual patients rather than estimating prevalence, with the demographic table shown both ways.

Specialist answered

Use NHANES wights and without weights and compare

Ruling

The comparison is not constructible for this cycle set, and this must go back to the specialist rather than be quietly dropped. Three reasons compound:

  1. The two-year cycles carry WTMEC2YR; P_ carries WTMECPRP, built on a 3.2-year basis. They are not on a common footing.
  2. The cycles are non-contiguous — 2011–2012 is absent and 2015–2016 is deliberately excluded. NHANES pooled-weight guidance covers contiguous cycles sharing a design stratification; there is no sanctioned formula for gapped sets.
  3. Until the §1 fix, two cycles overlap, and no weighting scheme can repair double-counted respondents.

What can honestly be offered instead: unweighted prediction work as the primary, plus within-cycle weighted descriptive estimates reported per cycle rather than pooled. That answers the spirit of the request — showing whether the sampled women differ from the population they were drawn from — without asserting a pooled weight that does not exist.
