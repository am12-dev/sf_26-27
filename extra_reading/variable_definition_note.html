NHANES osteoporosis screening study · variable definition query

Two variables needing a clinical decision

You flagged prior fracture and alcohol as measured in ways that may not carry clinical meaning. You were right about both. This note sets out exactly what the NHANES questionnaire offers for each, what each candidate definition would do to the numbers, and asks you to pick one.

1  Prior fracture

Currently in the model as any_prior_fracture · 233 positives (15.7%)

What the model uses today

A single yes/no flag: has this woman ever fractured her hip, spine or wrist, at any age. It is built from three questions (OSQ010A, OSQ010B, OSQ010C) and set to 1 if any of them is yes.

Your objection was that a childhood fracture says nothing about adult bone density, and the data confirms the contamination is substantial: of 178 women reporting a spine fracture, 67 report their most recent one before age 18. The median age at first spine fracture in this cohort is 24.

One thing I could not verify

If OSQ040xx does distinguish high-trauma from low-trauma fractures, it would let the variable be restricted to fragility fractures — which is what FRAX counts and presumably what you actually care about. I am not willing to assert that from the response pattern alone. Could you confirm from the NHANES osteoporosis codebook whether OSQ040 asks about the cause of the fracture? If it does, that changes the recommendation below.

The trade-off, stated plainly

The 5-year window is the strongest signal and the weakest evidence. The 10-year window keeps almost all of the signal (+19.6 vs +20.4 percentage points) with 57% more cases to estimate from. If you have no strong clinical preference, my suggestion would be to define the variable at 5 years as you asked, and additionally report the 10-year version as a sensitivity analysis — that way the headline follows your clinical judgement and the robustness question is answered before anyone asks it.

2  Alcohol

Currently in the model as alcohol_drinks_day · 470 of 1,484 missing (31.7%)

What the model uses today — and why it means less than it appears to

A single variable, ALQ130. You raised the absence of a time frame. There is in fact a 12-month window on the question, so that part is covered — but the variable has a different and more serious problem, and it produces the same practical result you predicted.

ALQ130 is the average number of drinks on the days she drank. It is not average daily intake. A woman who has four glasses of wine once a month and a woman who has four every single day both record ALQ130 = 4. The model currently cannot tell them apart.

Two defects, both fixable

1. The denominator is missing. Frequency is recorded separately (ALQ120Q + ALQ120U, or ALQ121 in later cycles) and is currently unused. Average daily intake needs frequency × quantity.

2. Non-drinkers are being filled in with the group median. Of the 470 missing values, 384 are confirmed non-drinkers — women who were never asked ALQ130 because they had already said they do not drink. The model fills their blank with the median of the drinkers, so several hundred non-drinkers are currently represented as light drinkers. Only 86 are genuinely unknown.

What the numbers say

As a continuous measure, alcohol carries essentially no signal in this cohort — every version sits within a couple of percentage points of nothing, in both directions. All of the signal is in the tail. At the FRAX threshold of three drinks a day the contrast is +24.9 percentage points, the largest of any variable examined in this study — but it rests on 20 women.

So the variable is not useless. It is mis-specified. Averaged across everyone it measures nothing; as a heavy-drinking flag it may measure something real, on very few people.

A coding bug I found while preparing this, worth knowing about

The pipeline blanks out NHANES “refused” and “don't know” codes, which include the numbers 7 and 9. But ALQ130 is a count of drinks, so a woman who genuinely reports 7 or 9 drinks a day has her answer deleted as though she had refused. It affects three women here — and they are exactly the heavy drinkers who carry whatever signal exists. This will be fixed regardless of which definition you choose.

What I need from you

Four decisions

Please pick one option per question

  1. Prior fracture — which window? Fracture in the last 5 years (46 women), the last 10 years (72), or from age 50 onwards (59)? I will report a second window as a sensitivity analysis whichever you choose.
  2. Does OSQ040 record how the fracture happened? If it separates low-trauma from high-trauma fractures, should the variable count only low-trauma ones?
  3. Alcohol — which definition? My reading is that D (binary, ≥ 3 drinks/day, FRAX-aligned) is the only version with real signal, and that C should be kept alongside it as the continuous measure so the null is on the record. Would you define heavy use differently for bone health — 2 a day, or a binge measure rather than a daily average?
  4. Is 20 women enough to report? An effect this size on 20 people will have a very wide confidence interval. Should it go in the model, or be reported as a descriptive finding only?
Already actioned from your last review

hysterectomy has been removed from the model. Your reasoning — that hysterectomy with the ovaries retained does not induce menopause — matches what the data showed independently: an adjusted odds ratio of 0.96 (0.68–1.42), and near-total overlap with the two variables kept, since only 6 of the 1,484 women report a bilateral oophorectomy without a hysterectomy.

steroid_use is queued for removal. NHANES records how long steroids were taken but never when, so your 5-year rule cannot be applied to it. Restricted to more than three months of use it flags 43 women with a contrast of +1.8 percentage points; beyond six months the contrast turns negative. It carries nothing.

any_arthritis is queued for replacement. It asks about arthritis of any type and is positive in 45.8% of the cohort. Rheumatoid arthritis specifically — the FRAX risk factor — can be isolated in five of the six cycles: 54 women, 3.6%, with a contrast of +8.8 percentage points.
